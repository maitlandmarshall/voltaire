Why Voltaire matters

Voltaire is not a mortal who received a tidy divine office. He is an exile who learned to edit memory, bargain for belief, and leave a single letter where an identity had been. His rank is young; his theology is not. Every new witness forces the same question: is the V a signature, a promise, or the first letter of a world being revised?

How to read this archive

The campaign record speaks in unequal voices: witnessed sessions, Voltaire’s own notes, historical sheets, later summaries, relic descriptions, and private encounter planning. This dossier preserves those layers rather than sanding them into one convenient history. The technical audit trail is kept separately in voltaire-source-ledger.md.

Record key

Witnessed Inferred Proposed Unresolved

Witnessed events and current mechanics are treated as canon. Inferred readings connect several records. Proposed material is an intention, doctrine, or encounter option awaiting play. Unresolved marks genuine contradiction.

A new god begins not with omnipotence, but with jurisdiction: the first place where reality allows his version to stand. Editorial thesis · artistic reconstruction

I The living contradiction

The Being Known as Voltaire

Voltaire is a Variant Human only in the grammatical sense that a ruin may still be called a house. He remembers himself as a Fey prince of a dim swamp kingdom, yet the Deck event that threw him into a desert stripped his Fey nature, his country, and much of the continuity between them. The present creature is Rogue, Archfey Warlock, self-worshipper, thief, archivist, and—since the Shadar-kai initiation—Divine Rank 1.

His current alignment is recorded as Chaotic Neutral. Older records call him Chaotic Good. Neither label reaches the central contradiction: he can offer light, protection, and recognition, yet his first divine following was made by replacing memories and hiding belief inside fine print. He wants freedom from the body as cage; his methods risk turning other minds into rooms of his own.

Voltaire’s mechanical patron remains the Archfey. His faith is Self Worship. Shar witnesses, limits, and facilitates parts of his ascent, but the archive does not name her as his warlock patron.

Present classification

Nature: formerly Fey; now Variant Human and nascent divinity.

Aspect: Rogue 5 (Thief) / Warlock 8 (Archfey), level 13.

Faith: Self Worship.

Rank: Divine Rank 1, gained 6 June 2026.

Disposition: Chaotic Neutral; restless, theatrical, acquisitive, capable of loyalty, and dangerously certain that authorship confers authority.

II What the witness retains

Appearance & Manifestations

Historical sheets describe white skin, eyes that do not blink, a frown painful enough to seem permanent, and the residue of impossible anatomies: a donkey tail, Nightgaunt skin, angelic and abyssal script. Current portraiture prefers a lean, dark-clad human silhouette. Both may be true. Disguise is trivial to him; consistency is not owed.

Flesh
The Shadar-kai carved a V into his chest and forehead with a dagger he offered. These are accession wounds, not decorative heraldry.
Eyes
The Robe of Eyes surrounds him with borrowed sight; at the Blood Temple, multiplying eyes became a V. Through Cornholio, vision once travelled “in reverse.”
Movement
He walks, climbs without penalty, vanishes through Misty Escape, and assumes dire wolf, giant octopus, or giant spider form.
Presence
Shadows may liquefy into tall umbral flowers. Ink and bark may age as though a fresh mark has always been present. A temple may darken without becoming more oppressive.
Voice
Ordinary speech can carry theatrical command; once, the word “Fall” sealed a rupture. At distance, a single word—“Machinations”—filled the Blood Temple.

Signs & omens

Attention: text acquires marginalia; a witness notices a V only after looking away. Favour: darkness becomes navigable rather than absent; forgotten details return with uncomfortable precision. Displeasure: agreements reveal the clauses no one read. Absence: his marks remain, but disagree about when they were made. The last four are theological extrapolations from witnessed motifs, not universal laws.

III Two drawings, often confused

The Swamp, the Desert & the Sun

Before the sand

He remembers a dim swamp kingdom: curved wood, mud, a throne, a father who ruled, and the civilising danger of visiting bards with gifts, wine, and song. Later notes reduce the homeland to ache—“the swamp is fleeting,” the family sigil missing, the throne too quiet. Titania, Oberon, and King Oponon appear as fragments and possibilities, not a stable genealogy.

The first drawing

A Deck of Many Things displaced the Fey prince into a desert and made him human—or “faux-human.” The most detailed retrospective names the King of Spades and Ace of Clubs and calls the desert Michaca; earlier raw testimony remembers only a card. What remains certain is the wound: kingdom, nature, and memory were taken together. He wandered for months. No record proves a trial, crime, judge, or deliberate exiler. “Exile” names the condition, not a sentence.

I’m Prince of the Swamp, not the fucking desert. Voltaire · retrospective account

The later Sun

A subsequent Sun draw brought extraordinary advancement and is associated with Voltaire’s warlock transformation. Its homebrew radiance once vaporised a demon who defaulted on a wager. On 16 August 2025, Voltaire fed or joined the card to a frog or toad; a natural 20 produced Robin, a white-light disciple who insists she is not an animal.

The card that remains

The current 2026 sheet still lists a Sun Card. The record does not explain whether the object reconstituted, the inventory lagged behind play, a copy persists, or “Sun Card” now denotes a bond rather than a possession. Robin’s personhood must not be reduced to the equipment line.

The recurring mistake is revealing: theologians want one card to explain every wound. Voltaire’s archive refuses them that economy.

IV Observed apotheosis

Divine Rank 1

Rank 0 · Belief by revision

On 16 August 2025, the Ink replaced two Fey creatures’ memories of their nature deity with memories of Voltaire. A fine-print clause made Shrek a third follower. The record marked Divine Rank 0. Its beginning was effective and coercive.

Six months · The Bloodweb country

In one combat round elsewhere, roughly six subjective Feywild months passed. Bloodweb spiders accepted him; daily bone rites continued while the surrounding region darkened.

The experiment · Concepts without life

With a voidbone pen he extracted darkness and light, then tried to create life. The attempt drained him; Shar stopped it. He stripped concepts from creatures and left V—or something of himself—inside their minds.

6 June 2026 · Flesh becomes scripture

About twenty Shadar-kai emerged. Their leader whipped Voltaire and carved V into his chest and forehead with the Shadow Dagger he offered. They admitted and dedicated themselves. The session record states: Divine Rank 1.

25 July 2026 · An office tested

He destroyed a hollow statue of Mask, laid the rubble on the Robe of Eyes, and occupied the place for six subjective months. Robin appeared; he bestowed the Blessing of V. Later, the Blood Temple answered at distance without the planned rite being performed.

What Rank 1 means here

A congregation can recognise him; his mark can persist in flesh, mind, place, and record; subjective months can become ritual territory; attention can arrive through a distant witness; and a blessing can be conferred, though its effect is undefined.

What it does not yet mean

No source grants omniscience, immortality, a universal domain, legendary resistance, rank-based armour, or automatic control of every V. The proposed Domain of Unbecoming did not answer mechanically. The first summit rite had not begun.

V Portfolio before doctrine

The Emerging Divine Portfolio

No divine domain has been mechanically ratified. These are jurisdictions inferred from repeated acts: what Voltaire changes, what petitioners might ask of him, and what each authority could demand in return.

Strong inference

Memory & Identity

Claim: the Ink transfers memory; the Crab Book remembers; concepts can be removed and replaced by V. Petition: “Let me keep the self the world is taking.” Obligation: distinguish restoration from authorship.

Corruption: remembrance becomes possession; forgetting becomes obedience.

Strong inference

Recognition & Authored Faith

Claim: self-worship, contractual belief, inscriptions of “Voltaire is divine,” and willing initiation all convert recognition into standing. Petition: “See me, and let my witness count.”

Corruption: agreement is treated as devotion, or attention as consent.

Strong inference

Thresholds & Exile

Claim: swamp to desert, Fey to human, rupture to Shadowfell, altered time, and the intended bridge between realms. Petition: safe passage for those whom home no longer recognises.

Corruption: every refuge becomes another departure gate.

Emerging · disputed

Light Within Darkness

Claim: stolen solar force, Robin as his sun, Voltaire as her moonlight, and darkness that can become less oppressive. Petition: light that does not erase the night sheltering it.

Corruption: duality becomes an excuse to enthrone every cruelty beside a mercy.

Claimed · unratified

Unbecoming

Claim: Voltaire proposed the Domain of Unbecoming; no response followed. The theme nevertheless fits lost Fey nature, bodily confinement, mutable identity, and concept-stripping. Petition: release from an imposed form.

Corruption: liberation becomes deletion.

Editorial synthesis

Inscription & Machination

Claim: living books, visitor ledgers, sigils that age into place, bargains, fine print, and the Blood Temple word. Petition: make the true account survive its author.

Corruption: the best-written version is mistaken for truth.

Editorial ruling: “portfolio” here describes accumulating narrative jurisdiction. It does not grant cleric domains, domain spells, or ownership of a cosmic concept. At Divine Rank 1, obligation may be the more reliable measure of divinity.

VI A network not yet born

The V & the Proposed Beacon Network

V is a simple mark

It is a calling card, scar, or remainder: placed in concept-stripped minds, cut into Voltaire’s flesh, and formed by eyes at the Blood Temple.

The follower sigil is ornate

Triangle beyond square; seven-point outline; eyes; pale light-ink and dark filigree. It was carved into the aspen and drawn in the archive.

A beacon is editorial

The records describe a planned rite that would drive V through the follower sigil and call connected marks. “Beacon network” is a useful model, not established vocabulary.

Canon boundary. The Blood Temple answered before the summit rite. Therefore it proves remote manifestation, not network activation. If the network grows, no source yet says whether Voltaire alone will answer.

VII What has actually happened

Powers & Divine Phenomena

The Ink of Unbeing

A Book of Hollow Binding and voidbone pen can transfer memory, bless forgetfulness, or curse a subject with eternal recall. In practice, the Ink has rewritten faith and altered what creatures know themselves to be.

Relic-mediated · morally hazardous

Reader of Every Tongue

Exposure to the Books of Vile Darkness and Exalted Deeds left all-language comprehension implicit. His current sheet lists “All,” alongside Abyssal, Common, Elvish, and Thieves’ Cant.

Persistent character capability

The Book That Remembers

Celsus—the crab-book bound to his tail—consumes skin and blood, retains accounts, and issues prophecy. Voltaire can smell a book’s authorship or significance with unnerving directness.

Observed relic ecology

Borrowed Sun

The Sun Card carried a 6d8 radiant blast in homebrew notes and vaporised a wager-defaulting demon. Its later union with Robin changed the meaning of the power from possession to relationship.

Historic · card status unresolved

Fall, Fear & Glamour

The word “Fall” sealed a dangerous rupture. Archfey magic lets him charm or frighten, disappear into disguise, become invisible when struck, and move by mist rather than road.

One singular word; otherwise confirmed mechanics

Feral Transformation

Once per long rest he may become a dire wolf, giant octopus, or giant spider while retaining mind, speech, and limited verbal spellcasting. The tower ascent proved the spider form more than spectacle.

Confirmed adventuring trait

Concept Surgery

He extracted darkness and light as concepts, attempted life, and left V in emptied minds. The failed creation drained him until Shar intervened; mastery is therefore neither complete nor safe.

Witnessed once · limits demonstrated

Attention at a Distance

Through Cornholio’s reverse eye, Voltaire’s word and mark reached the Blood Temple. He has also conferred the undefined Blessing of V. Whether either effect was conscious, repeatable, or dependent on consent remains unknown.

Divine phenomenon · mechanism unknown

Powers not yet established

General soul manipulation; automatic travel between every marked place; creation of living beings; command of all Fey or Shadar-kai; resurrection; invulnerability; true omnipresence; and any mechanical package attached solely to Divine Rank 1.

VIII The dangerous arithmetic

Soul Coins, Worship & Divine Economy

Voltaire has traded in soul coins. The archive does not therefore make him lord of souls. His divine economy is better evidenced in memory, recognition, signatures, and acts that teach reality to expect him. His sheet calls his faith Self Worship, and the sessions show him actively recruiting recognition; they do not yet show how he receives an independent prayer he did not arrange.

WagerSixteen soul coins won from a demon.
DefaultThe demon reneged and was vaporised by solar force.
InscriptionThe coins were reported imbued as sketches in the Crab Book.
SpendOne later paid as 500 gp, with 450 gp returned.

Unresolved account: a coin embedded in a living book later behaved as spendable currency. This may be stale narration, reversible inscription, a different coin, or a property of Celsus. No record chooses among them. There is likewise no evidence that Voltaire can consume, redeem, purify, free, or transform souls in general.

Existing circles of devotion

The Rewritten First

Two Fey whose former deity was removed from memory, plus Shrek through fine print. They prove belief can be engineered; they do not prove it should be.

Status: established, ethically compromised.

The Shadar-kai Initiates

Roughly twenty witnesses who whipped, marked, and dedicated themselves during the accession. Their doctrine, previous loyalties, and expectations remain unstated.

Status: established congregation.

Those Who Sign

The archive visitor book repeats “Voltaire is divine” and ends “sign here.” A future sect might treat signatures as recognition; no signer is yet proven a devotee.

Status: plausible accidental cult.

Rites that already have precedent

  • Cutting or drawing V as admission, signature, or remainder.
  • Bone rites offered daily among Bloodweb spiders, without a recorded creed.
  • Writing divinity into a public record and inviting another hand to sign.
  • Holding light and darkness together: Robin as sun, Voltaire as moonlight.
  • 6 June marks accession; 25 July marks blessing and distant manifestation. Neither is yet a declared holy day.

Taboos a responsible cult would need

  • No memory altered to manufacture consent.
  • No contract treated as worship unless the clause was understood.
  • No soul coin romanticised; it is a prison and currency at once.
  • No ornate sigil reduced to V, nor every V claimed as his property.
Worship may strengthen a new god. More dangerously, it may tell him which version of himself he is expected to become. Comparative theology · strong inference

IX The people who make the god legible

Relationships of Consequence

Shar

Witness · limiter · facilitator

She received Voltaire after the rupture, figures in his trial geometry, and stopped his failed creation of life. He wants access, recognition, and perhaps a road through her realm; what she wants from his ascent is not recorded.

Robin

Disciple · sun · independent person

Born from the Sun Card and an amphibian transformation, Robin later became his declared guiding light; he offered to be her moonlight and granted the Blessing of V. She wants to be understood as a person, not an object or animal.

Cornholio

Ally · signer · possible conduit

A party member, consort of Shar, and signer of the visitor book. Voltaire later saw through his eye in reverse as the Blood Temple changed. Both need to learn whether this was connection, consent, possession, or accident.

Greg

Catalyst · accomplice · witness

Greg used haste so Voltaire could survive reading and transcribing both the Vile and Exalted books, embedding cosmic good and evil in him. Voltaire owes his widened language and dangerous interior duality partly to Greg’s intervention.

Glasya

Archdevil · fascination · affiliation

Summoned, curious, and connected to his intended Trial; the current sheet calls Voltaire an affiliate of “Glasher.” She offers attention from a power that understands contracts too well for his fine print to remain a private joke.

Celsus

Companion · archive · appetite

The Crab Book is bound to Voltaire’s tail, fed with skin and blood, and capable of memory and prophecy. Voltaire wants a record that survives him; Celsus’s wants are mostly expressed as hunger and text.

The Shadar-kai Initiates

Congregation · makers of rank

About twenty revealed themselves, marked him, and dedicated themselves. He wanted recognition; what they expect from their new god—and what old loyalty their rite complicates—remains the central debt of Rank 1.

The Unresolved Fey Court

Origin · inheritance · future conflict

Titania, Oberon, King Oponon, and Corellon recur as place, fragment, or intended confrontation. None is proved parent, sovereign, or exiler. Voltaire wants his stolen story back; the court may dispute the version he returns with.

Mask: Voltaire destroyed a hollow statue and occupied its place, but no record confirms he inherited Mask’s office. Bloodweb spiders: they accepted him and his bone rites; worship and pact are unproven.

X Inventory as autobiography

Relics & Possessions

Celsus, the Crab Book

Form: living, tail-bound book; also titled Machinations & Actions: 5e—Player’s Handbook. Property: consumes flesh and blood, remembers, prophesies. Meaning: an archive that is bodily, hungry, and difficult to discard.

Does Celsus preserve Voltaire, or slowly replace him with the account?

Ink of Unbeing & Voidbone Pen

Origin: the Book of Hollow Binding tradition. Property: transfers memory; may grant forgetting or eternal recall; has manipulated concepts. Cost: agency, identity, and physical depletion.

The pen was manifested and used; whether it remains a carried object is unresolved.

The Sun Card

Property: advancement and a 6d8 radiant blast in historic notes. Meaning: stolen daylight, wager enforcement, then relationship through Robin. Danger: treating a person as the property that preceded her.

Robin exists, yet the 2026 inventory still lists the card.

Shadow Daggers +2

Property: paired, +11 to hit, 1d4+6 piercing, thrown 20/60; each can return or be summoned. Meaning: the thief’s certainty that what leaves his hand remains his. One was offered for the accession scars.

A separate Sharite ceremonial dagger may or may not be one of the pair.

Robe of Eyes & Mask Rubble

Property: current attuned relic of expanded sight. Origin of burden: the fragments of hollow Mask were placed upon it. Meaning: observation bearing the remains of an unoccupied identity.

No effect from the rubble has yet been recorded.

The Sixteen Soul Coins

Origin: a demon wager. Property: infernal currency; reported as sketches imbued in Celsus. One later spent as 500 gp with change. Meaning: the obscenity of reducing a soul to denomination.

Number, location, and recoverability no longer reconcile.

Other burdens: two stolen volumes of Glimpse within the Abyss; Dark Moon Heresy, absorbed by the crab-tail and unreliable as theology; a Headband of Intellect and Ring of Protection, both currently attuned. The Vile and Exalted books altered him, but are not listed as his possessions.

XIII Use only with table ratification

Divine Rank 1 Encounter Profile

This overlay is a narrative instrument, not a replacement stat block. Run Voltaire from his confirmed adventuring sheet. Add only the authorities the scene has earned, and make every divine effect expose an obligation or limit.

Observed basis

Inscription of Attention

When Voltaire deliberately marks a creature, object, or place with V, the mark may hold memory of his attention. It does not automatically grant control, travel, or sight. Ask who consented, what was removed, and what the mark now remembers.

Observed once

Witness at a Distance

A signed ally or spiritually entangled witness may become a momentary line of sight and voice, as Cornholio did. Treat this as a scene trigger with consequences—not a reusable surveillance action—until play defines consent and range.

Relic-mediated

Revision of Remembrance

Through the Ink and an appropriate ritual, Voltaire can propose transferring, suppressing, or fixing memory. The subject, cost, source memory, and destination must be explicit. A saving throw alone does not resolve the moral fact.

Mindscape only

Editorial Authority

Within his Head-Space, module records frame Voltaire as a lair-like presence who can alter gravity, first blood, redaction, and continuity from a throne. These are private mind-domain encounter rules, not proven powers in ordinary reality.

How an encounter should feel

Ally

Useful, fast, and personally invested—then asks whether the party will sign what they just agreed to remember.

Patron

Offers passage, a recovered memory, or the right to be recognised. Payment is an obligation, not merely gold.

Rival

Wins by changing which account survives. Defeating him means preserving independent witnesses.

Unknown

Show effects first: an old sigil, impossible marginalia, a room whose darkness is suddenly gentle.

Object of worship

Put the believer’s need before doctrine. Voltaire must choose whether to answer, exploit, or refuse it.

Mythic scene

Pressure identity, consent, and consequence. Raw damage is secondary to what a character permits the story to call them.

Regional & metaphysical effects

Sustained presence

Witnessed precedent: subjective time dilates and local darkness changes character. Proposed limit: the effect ends with his attention.

Shrine or dormant beacon

Names and promises become hard to forget. A true title spoken there may echo across marks; a broken promise may return as hostile marginalia. All are interpretive.

Injury, death or absence

V marks disagree about their age; followers remember incompatible versions of the same rite. At death, each mark may retain a different “last” Voltaire. Not yet witnessed.

Locked option: do not grant “Network Step,” automatic beacon travel, or network-wide perception until the summit rite is actually performed and its answer is resolved in play.

XIV Do not resolve for neatness

Secrets, Mysteries & Adventure Hooks

  1. Who—or what—benefited when a Fey prince, his nature, and his memories were separated?
  2. Were the King of Spades and Ace of Clubs truly the cards of exile, or a later attempt to make chaos legible?
  3. What remains of the Sun Card after Robin, and why does the inventory still name it?
  4. Did the Shadar-kai recognise Voltaire, manufacture him, or admit a thing already waiting behind V?
  5. Was hollow Mask an empty office, a warning, or bait designed for a thief who cannot resist vacancy?
  6. Does the Blood Temple answer Voltaire—or did something use his new mark to answer through him?
  7. Can the Ink restore identity without claiming editorial ownership of the restored person?
  8. If Rank 2 comes from wider worship, which part of Voltaire must become simpler enough for a religion to repeat?

Ten campaign-ready hooks

1 · The Signer Who Never Signed

A name appears beneath “sign here” in the archive book. Its owner has never visited—and is beginning to remember serving Voltaire.

2 · Sixteen Minus One

The soul behind the spent coin returns with a receipt for 450 gp and a claim that Celsus still owns the change in years of life.

3 · The False V

A Fey faction brands prisoners with crude Vs and attributes every coercion to the new god. Destroying the cult may also erase real evidence of his origin.

4 · What the Aspen Remembers

The aged sigil opens one night, not as a portal but as a memory of the swamp kingdom. Someone inside remembers Voltaire as its betrayer.

5 · Robin’s Second Sun
